01 — Executive Summary

The $13B Administrative Crisis Nobody Has Solved

Prior authorization (PA) is the single most disruptive administrative bottleneck in U.S. specialty healthcare. Physicians spend an average of 13 hours per week managing authorizations — time that should be spent treating patients. The U.S. healthcare system burns an estimated $13B annually on administrative overhead, and 94% of physicians report that PA directly delays patient care. Despite decades of digitization efforts, the core decision-making problem remains entirely manual.

PriorIQ Health is building the intelligence layer between providers and payers. We automate the four manual steps of every prior authorization — requirement determination, clinical documentation extraction, submission, and denial appeal — using an AI-native platform purpose-built for specialty care. Our starting market is independent rheumatology practices, where 100% of biologic therapies require authorization, denial rates exceed 20%, and therapy values reach $80K per year per patient.

The timing is not coincidental. CMS finalized a mandate (CMS-0057-F) requiring all major payers to expose standardized FHIR prior authorization APIs by January 2027. This creates a forcing function that has never existed before. Combined with enterprise-grade clinical AI now available, labor shortages, margin compression, and the highest physician burnout rates on record, the conditions for a category-defining company are present today.

03 — Market Timing

Why This Product Could Not Have Existed Three Years Ago

Three structural shifts converge in 2026 to create a narrow and highly favorable window. All three are independently powerful; together they are category-defining.

2024

Enterprise-Grade Clinical AI Becomes Available

Large language models reach clinical-grade extraction accuracy for structured medical records, lab values, and ICD/CPT coding — making AI-assisted PA feasible for the first time at production scale. Claude Sonnet-class models process clinical documents with ≥95% structured field accuracy at ~$0.015 per PA document.

January 2026

CMS-0057-F Core Provisions Go Live

CMS's Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule activates key payer obligations: faster decision timelines, mandatory denial reasons with specific criteria, and the start of the transition to structured electronic prior authorization workflows. Every major commercial insurer is now legally required to accelerate and justify decisions.

January 2027

FHIR Da Vinci APIs Mandated for Prior Authorization

CMS requires major payers to expose standardized FHIR-based Prior Authorization APIs (CRD, DTR, PAS). This creates machine-readable payer criteria, structured denial data, and programmatic submission infrastructure — the exact data layer PriorIQ is building its intelligence network on top of.

2026–2028+

Accelerating Tailwinds: Labor, Margin, Burnout

Administrative staff shortages are forcing practices to reduce headcount. Margin compression from payer rate cuts makes automation ROI immediate. AMA surveys show physician PA burden at all-time highs. The incentive to adopt a system that provably reduces PA time by 50%+ has never been stronger.

05 — Product

Three Layers. Each One Compounds the Last.

PriorIQ is built as a three-layer architecture where every interaction generates data and every outcome improves future decisions. The initial product delivers immediate workflow ROI. The data network is the durable moat.

Layer 1

Authorization Copilot

AI reads EHR documents, extracts 20–35 required clinical fields per authorization (diagnosis codes, disease activity scores, prior treatment failures, lab values), matches to payer criteria, and scores approval probability. Staff review and submit in under 5 minutes.

Layer 2

Denial Prediction Engine

Trained on historical authorization outcomes across payers, drugs, and indications. Flags likely denial reasons before submission. Recommends additional clinical evidence that shifts the approval probability. Gets smarter with every processed PA.

Layer 3 — The Moat

Appeal Intelligence

Transforms denial reason codes into optimized appeal packages using historical appeal success patterns. Tracks peer-to-peer review outcomes. Creates a longitudinal learning loop that competitors cannot replicate without years of outcome data.

Technical Architecture

The POC phase deploys a three-layer stack: secure HIPAA-compliant document ingestion (AWS + KMS + BAAs with Anthropic, Supabase, Twilio), an AI extraction engine using Claude API with RAG over a structured payer criteria knowledge base (50+ payer × drug × indication combinations), and a Playwright-based browser automation layer covering 3 major payer portals. Full FHIR Da Vinci API integration (CRD, DTR, PAS) replaces browser automation in Months 8–14, timed to the January 2027 CMS mandate.

10 — Risk Landscape

Known Risks, Managed Deliberately

PriorIQ operates with a live risk register reviewed at every sprint retrospective. The two existential risks at the POC stage are product-market validation (R-01) and AI accuracy (R-04). Both are managed through a deliberate sequencing rule: no build before LOIs are signed, and humans remain in the loop in v1 so that accuracy issues never reach the patient record.

R-01 — No LOIs secured by Week 4

Mitigation: Do not start build before LOIs. Pivot specialty or pricing if 20 interviews yield zero LOIs. This is the pre-condition for all subsequent investment of time and capital.

High

R-04 — AI extraction accuracy below 85%

Mitigation: Human review step built into the UI for v1. Staff approves every submission before it is sent. Accuracy improves with each PA processed as the model learns from corrections.

High

R-03 — Payer portals block browser automation

Mitigation: FHIR API integration by Month 14 eliminates browser automation dependency entirely. Short-term: use official payer developer portal sandbox environments and monitor ToS changes.

Medium

R-05 — PHI data breach / HIPAA violation

Mitigation: HIPAA-grade AWS infrastructure from Day 1. BAAs with all PHI-touching vendors (Anthropic, Supabase, AWS). External pentest in Week 13. Cyber insurance by Month 6. Vanta continuous controls monitoring.

Medium

R-10 — Seed round fails / insufficient runway

Mitigation: Bootstrap to $20K MRR before raising. Apply to YC S2026. SBIR Phase I ($150–300K) as non-dilutive bridge option. Revenue-first approach makes seed raise optional if traction is strong.

Low–Medium
11 — Defensibility & Exit

The Data Network Is the Moat. The Software Is the Vehicle.

Every prior authorization processed by PriorIQ generates a data point: the clinical profile submitted, the payer criteria matched, the approval probability scored, and the outcome recorded. Over time, this longitudinal outcome dataset — spanning payers, drugs, specialties, and practice types — becomes extremely difficult to replicate. A competitor starting today would need years of real-world PA outcomes to match the predictive accuracy of a network that has been learning since launch.

Layer 1 — Easiest to Copy

Technology Features

Software UI, extraction prompts, and workflow automation can be replicated within months by a well-funded team.

Layer 2 — Moderately Defensible

Workflow Integration

Deep embedding in practice workflows, EHR integrations, and staff habits creates switching friction that takes 6–18 months to replicate.

Layer 3 — Extremely Difficult to Replicate

Payer Intelligence Network

Longitudinal authorization outcomes, denial patterns, appeal success rates, and payer behavior data by specialty. Cannot be purchased or scraped — it must be earned through real PA volume.

The PriorIQ Intelligence Network is the long-term moat. Every prior authorization processed — regardless of outcome — contributes structured data to four interconnected intelligence layers. Over time, the network's predictive accuracy compounds in ways that competitors building from scratch cannot match. This is what makes PriorIQ a data business, not just a workflow tool.

Why the Network Becomes Unassailable

Each additional practice joining PriorIQ contributes new payer × drug × indication × outcome data combinations. The denial prediction model retrains on every correction.
